# Open-weight hubs and new local models (as of 1 November 2026)

Scope note: dates below are taken from the cited page. “Updated N days/months ago” is relative to pages fetched on 1 November 2026. Anything the sources place before August 2026 is marked background. Where two pages give different numbers, both are listed. Numbers that were not on the page are not filled in.

## What are the main open-weight distribution sites in current use?

### Takeaway

Weights still land first on Hugging Face, with ModelScope as the stated alternate when Hugging Face is unreachable. LM Studio, Unsloth, mlx-community, and Ollama are not new labs; they are the catalogs and quant builds people actually run locally. No new general-purpose weight host showed up in the 2026 pages reviewed here. What did show up are app catalogs (LM Studio, Unsloth Desktop) and a rewritten ModelScope client.

### Cited Findings

- Hugging Face remains the place labs point to for original checkpoints. The 16 October 2026 Hugging Face post puts Reflection Beam on the Hub (BF16, FP8, NVFP4). The 15 October 2026 Hugging Face post puts Meta Muse Spark Open on the Hub as `meta-models/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B` and `…-NVFP4`, and points GGUF at `ggml-org/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B-GGUF` and an MLX quant at `mlx-community/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B-oQ3e`. DeepSeek’s card for `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017` is on the Hub and says the weights are MIT. Qwen’s GitHub README (news lines dated 12 and 14 August 2026) says official weights are on the Hugging Face Hub and on ModelScope. Hugging Face’s own MLX doc describes the Hub as the place to filter and load MLX weights, and names mlx-community as the converting community. A Hub filter `apps=lmstudio` lists GGUF repos tagged for LM Studio. — [Hugging Face, Beam, 16 Oct 2026](https://huggingface.co/blog/reflection-beam); [Hugging Face, Muse Spark Open, 15 Oct 2026](https://huggingface.co/blog/meta-muse-spark-open); [DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017); [Qwen3.8 README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8/blob/main/README.md); [Using MLX at Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/mlx); [mlx-community org](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community); [Hub models with apps=lmstudio](https://huggingface.co/models?apps=lmstudio)

- LM Studio’s model catalog (`lmstudio.ai/models`, sorted by recently updated on the fetch) is a curated list inside the desktop app, split into “Local — Available to download” and “Cloud — Available in LM Studio Cloud”. The fetched page header says “All models 57”, “Local 49”, “Cloud 9” (those three counts are what the page prints; 49+9 is not 57, and the page does not explain the gap). Cloud rows are labeled “LM Studio Cloud • US-hosted, ZDR”; the catalog page does not define ZDR. The same fetch’s newest local rows are LFM3 (updated 4 days ago), Flint (10 days), and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL (16 days). Beam and Muse Spark Open are cloud-only on that page (updated 15 days ago). The site footer also has a nav item “LM Studio Hub Beta”; this pass did not open a separate Hub product page, so what that beta adds beyond the catalog is not established. — [LM Studio catalog, recently updated](https://lmstudio.ai/models?sort=updated)

- Unsloth publishes GGUF (and some NVFP4/FP8) conversions on the Hub under `unsloth/`, and ships Unsloth Desktop. The changelog dates Desktop’s introduction to 11 August 2026 and says it supports MLX and GGUF. The 14 August 2026 entry says Qwen3.8-27B runs via “Unsloth Dynamic GGUFs”. The 28 September 2026 entry says “ModelScope model downloading is now available for users who can't use Hugging Face”. The 19 October 2026 entry documents DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 GGUFs; the 30 October 2026 entry says those were reissued as “Unsloth Dynamic v3.1 GGUFs (preview)”. The Hub org page, fetched with this research, shows `unsloth/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017-GGUF` updated 1 day ago and labeled 748B. — [Unsloth changelog](https://unsloth.ai/docs/new/changelog); [unsloth on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/models)

- mlx-community is a Hugging Face organization, not a separate website. Its org page says it hosts pre-converted weights for Apple Silicon for mlx-lm, mlx-swift-examples, mlx-vlm, and mlx-audio. The fetched org page prints “models 5,816” and “Team members 6,019”. A different activity URL in the same search prints “18,070” at the top of the org header; this note does not resolve which member count is current. Recent model rows on the org page include a Flint collection (`mlx-community/flint-oQ2e`, `flint-oQ2.5e`, `flint-oQ2.7e`, `flint-oQ3e`, each “40B”, “Updated 4 days ago”) and `mlx-community/Qwen3.8-27B-OptiQ-4bit` (“27B”, “Updated 1 day ago”). The Muse Spark Open Hugging Face post (15 October 2026) says the model “is available in oMLX and mlx-lm today” and gives the repo id `mlx-community/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B-oQ3e`. oMLX is named there as a runner with a download UI; this pass did not find a page that establishes oMLX as a public multi-lab hub. — [mlx-community](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community); [Hugging Face, Muse Spark Open, 15 Oct 2026](https://huggingface.co/blog/meta-muse-spark-open)

- Ollama’s library (`ollama.com/library` and `ollama.com/search?o=newest`) is a pull-by-name catalog. Entries are either local tags (a size such as `1.7b` or `30b`, plus pull counts) or marked `cloud`. The newest local names on the fetch include `lfm3` (1.7b, “Updated 18 hours ago” on the library sort, “2d” on search), `flint` (40b, “1w”), `muse-glimmer` (30b), `nemotron-3.5-lightning` (30b), `qwen3.8` (27b), and `gemma4`. `beam`, `muse-spark-open`, `deepseek-v4.1-flash`, `glm-5.3`, and `minimax-m3.1-flash` are marked cloud on those pages. The Ollama blog (5 June 2026, background) says Ollama 0.30 adds GGUF compatibility through llama.cpp “on top of” an MLX engine on Apple Silicon. The 10 August 2026 blog post says Muse Glimmer is “accelerated by Ollama's MLX engine”. The 11 August 2026 post adds Nemotron 3.5 Lightning. — [Ollama library, newest](https://ollama.com/library?sort=newest); [Ollama search, newest](https://ollama.com/search?o=newest); [Ollama blog](https://ollama.com/blog)

- ModelScope (`modelscope.cn` / `modelscope.ai`) is the Chinese model community. Its home page says it was founded in June 2022 by the Institute for Intelligent Computing together with the CCF Open Source Development Committee, and describes itself as an open-source model community and a “Model-as-a-Service” platform. The home page fetched for this note lists dated cards including “Nanbeige4.3-33B 2026.10.31” and “Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B 2026.10.16”. Qwen’s README tells readers who cannot use Hugging Face to use ModelScope, including `modelscope download`. The `modelscope-hub` GitHub repo (created 2026-05-28, Apache-2.0 on the repo) is a new Python SDK/CLI for that existing hub, not a new hub. Its README claims “100K+ models and datasets”. Unsloth’s 28 September 2026 changelog is the English-language desktop path onto ModelScope. — [ModelScope home](https://www.modelscope.cn/home); [modelscope-hub README](https://github.com/modelscope/modelscope_hub); [Qwen3.8 README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.8/blob/main/README.md); [Unsloth changelog, 28 Sep 2026](https://unsloth.ai/docs/new/changelog)

- No page in this pass documented a new 2026 weight host that sits alongside Hugging Face and ModelScope as a place labs upload original checkpoints. The new distribution surfaces are quant orgs and apps on top of those two: `ggml-org` GGUF repos, `unsloth` GGUFs, `mlx-community` MLX, LM Studio’s catalog (and an unopened “LM Studio Hub Beta” label), Unsloth Desktop, and Ollama’s library plus its cloud tier.

### Inferences

- For a local coding machine, the practical split is: original weights on Hugging Face (or ModelScope if that host is blocked), GGUF from Unsloth or ggml-org, MLX from mlx-community, and one-click pulls from the LM Studio catalog or `ollama pull`. LM Studio Cloud and Ollama Cloud rows are hosted inference, not a local weight download.
- “Updated N days ago” on LM Studio and Ollama is a catalog touch date, not a proof of the original release date.

### Gaps

- LM Studio Hub Beta was only a footer label. No description of it was fetched.
- oMLX was only described inside the Muse Spark Open post. No independent product page was fetched.
- The mlx-community member-count conflict (6,019 vs 18,070) was not resolved.
- ModelScope’s “100K+” figure is the client README’s claim; it was not checked against a live count.
- Nanbeige4.3-33B appears only as a 31 October 2026 title on the ModelScope home page. Lab, license, parameter breakdown, and GGUF/MLX were not on that snippet.

## Which notable open-weight models shipped in roughly August–November 2026, especially for local coding?

### Takeaway

The releases that match a local coding box (about 30B dense, or a small-active MoE) and have a sourced size are Meta Muse Glimmer (10 August, 30B, Apache-2.0, GGUF published), Qwen3.8-27B (14 August, 27B on the card, GGUF and an MLX quant exist; license not on the card body), NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning (Ollama post 11 August, 30B total / 3B active), and Recursive Flint (updates index 21 October, about 40B total / 2.5B active, Apache-2.0, community MLX quants exist). October’s headline MoEs — Muse Spark Open, Beam, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, MiniMax M3.1 Flash — are coding models with GGUF or MLX in some cases, but their total sizes are hundreds of billions and the pages that quote them do not agree on a single parameter count.

## What license, safety, or distribution controversy in that window matters for local weights?

### Takeaway

Two issues show up in primary or near-primary pages: Chinese labs registering open-weight releases under a new export-control draft, and a split between permissive licenses (Apache 2.0, MIT) and custom licenses with revenue or “model as a service” conditions. The custom-license details below come from a 28 October 2026 survey that claims to have read the license files; those files were not re-opened in this pass, so the clause text should be treated as that survey’s reading. Muse Spark Open’s license is the clearest local-runner gap: the welcome posts read here never name it.

### Cited Findings

- South China Morning Post reports that DeepSeek and MiniMax “have completed export registration procedures and proceeded with open-source releases and overseas licensing activities”, after a Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology draft proposed adding export-control restrictions to 23 AI-related technologies, “covering large language models, AI agents and computing clusters”, with some registration provisions “in immediate effect”. The search snippet does not include the article’s publication date, only “Thursday evening” and “last week”. A separate secondary article says the commerce and science ministries proposed the registration rules on 16 October 2026 and that a model above 2 trillion parameters or 10^26 FLOPs would need registration, with an open-source path described as a faster lane. That threshold was not checked against a ministry PDF. — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/policy/article/3371880/deepseek-minimax-secure-open-source-licenses-days-after-new-china-export-rules-state-media); [AI Lately, “China's Open-Weight Labs Go Quiet”](https://ailately.com/articles/china-open-weight-labs-go-quiet)

- Permissive releases that a local user can treat as ordinary open weights, on the pages actually opened: Beam is Apache 2.0 (Hugging Face, 16 October 2026). Muse Glimmer is Apache 2.0 (Meta, 10 August 2026, and the model card). Flint is Apache 2.0 (Recursive’s Flint post). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017’s card says MIT. The tracker also prints `apache-2.0` for Granite Switch 4.1 and Hy-Memory-30B-A3B, and `mit` for the DeepSeek repo. — [Hugging Face Beam post](https://huggingface.co/blog/reflection-beam); [Meta Glimmer post](https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-glimmer-open-agentic-model); [Muse Glimmer card](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B); [Flint post](https://recursive.com/updates/introducing-recursive-flint); [DeepSeek card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017); [Optinest tracker](https://optinest.de/data/trackers/open-weights-releases)

### Inferences

- For someone running weights on their own machine, Apache 2.0 and MIT (Glimmer, Flint, Beam, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017) do not add a revenue negotiation. The models where a local download might still be legally gated or non-commercial are the ones whose license file was not opened: Muse Spark Open (wiki: gated, custom license), MiniMax M3.1 Flash (wiki: community license, non-commercial base), and the large Qwen3.8 / Kimi / GLM flagships (secondary survey only). Qwen3.8-27B is the important local unknown: the card page fetched did not show a license.
- The China export story matters as distribution risk for future DeepSeek and MiniMax drops, not as a statement that current MIT/Apache files on the Hub were withdrawn. No page read here said the 1017 weights had been taken down.

### Gaps

- Muse Spark Open’s license file was not fetched. The Hugging Face welcome post does not name it.
- Qwen3.8-27B’s license was not on the fetched card.
- The SCMP article date and the MOFCOM draft’s exact thresholds were not verified from the ministry text.
- MiniMax, StepFun, Mistral Timestral, Liquid LFM2.5, NVIDIA OpenMDW, and the large Qwen/Kimi/GLM custom licenses were not opened. The wiki and Frontier Wire are the only sources for those clause details.
- No primary page in this pass documented a safety recall, a malware incident in a quant repo, or a Hugging Face ban of a major coding model between August and 1 November 2026. That absence is “not found”, not a claim that nothing happened.
